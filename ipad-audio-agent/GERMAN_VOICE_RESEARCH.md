# German voice options for Sound Flux

Research date: 24 September 2026. Scope: a commercial German-speaking companion for older adults, with local iPad inference preferred. The current test device is an M1 iPad Pro; M4 performance is not a substitute for testing that device. This is a documentation and licensing review, not a listening comparison or vendor quotation. Product priorities below are recommendations, not measured vendor rankings.

**Recommendation:** evaluate an established offline iOS supplier alongside our existing local engines. Acapela is the first procurement candidate; ReadSpeaker is the second. Use ElevenLabs as an optional cloud quality reference. Investigate Chatterbox separately because of unusually relevant German older-adult evidence, but do not assume its current iPad conversion can deliver that experience.

**Evidence relevant to our audience:** the SOVIA paper reports a listening comparison with 15 participants aged 64–89 and seven German TTS alternatives. Chatterbox VoiceCloning ranked first, followed by Chatterbox Multilingual, then Thorsten DDC. This supports including Chatterbox in an audition. It does not compare every supplier here, prove a loneliness benefit, or benchmark local iPad inference; its architecture uses a self-hosted backend. Model versions, speaker recordings and playback setup affect transferability. [Author-uploaded paper, section 4.5](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/405205481_SOVIA_A_Social_Voice_Assistant_Architecture_Based_on_a_Large_Language_Model_to_Reduce_Loneliness_among_Older_Adults_for_a_Participatory_Field_Trial), [research dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/18467395).

**Other open models:** Qwen3-TTS 0.6B supports German and is Apache-2.0, but this research has not validated its full runtime, voice rights, memory or latency on our iPad. Keep it as a later engineering experiment. XTTS-v2's public model license is noncommercial and does not meet our requirement. Supertonic remains a useful current comparator, but its OpenRAIL-M medical-advice exclusion and downstream contractual obligations matter if the product moves toward care-related guidance. [Qwen model](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-0.6B-Base), [XTTS license](https://huggingface.co/coqui/XTTS-v2/blob/main/LICENSE.txt), [Supertonic license](https://huggingface.co/Supertone/supertonic-3/blob/main/LICENSE).

**Product and market judgment:** a family-oriented companion should offer a recognizable, respectful voice and simple controls; users should not have to choose model brands. A care-provider pilot additionally benefits from predictable deployment, support, a stable voice identity, and clear contractual responsibility. These are reasons to request supplier evaluations, not proof that a proprietary voice sounds better. Local operation preserves our offline proposition; a cloud option changes that proposition and introduces network dependence and recurring usage costs.

Compare total cost over the expected product lifetime: integration, QA, voice rights, SDK fees, unit royalties, updates, support and inference. For illustration, 1,000 users receiving five minutes of synthesized speech daily produce 150,000 minutes per 30-day month. Request cloud quotes at that volume, and offline quotes for 100/1,000/10,000 installations. These are planning assumptions, not a usage forecast. Specify the expected share of German/Austrian German, commercial distribution channel, model updates and customer retention period.

**Smallest useful evaluation:** audition four candidates before adding more production engines: current Pocket/Supertonic, one Acapela or ReadSpeaker offline package, and optionally a cloud reference. Add a separate Chatterbox recording comparison using a commercially authorized German speaker. Keep the text and speaker volume consistent, randomize order, and run a formative test with 8–12 intended users; that sample informs design and does not establish population-level superiority.

Use greetings, interruptions, misunderstandings, dates, prices, names and regional vocabulary. For example: “Guten Morgen, Frau Weiß. Möchten Sie erst einmal in Ruhe ankommen?”, “Entschuldigung, ich habe Sie nicht richtig verstanden.”, “Am Donnerstag, dem 24. September, um Viertel nach neun.” and “Das kostet zwölf Euro fünfzig.” Test umlauts, compound words and local names. Let users choose du/Sie and pace. Avoid assuming that a particular gender or uniformly slower speech suits every older adult.

Measure first-listen comprehension, respectful tone, willingness to listen for ten minutes, pronunciation errors, skipped/repeated words, total response delay and successful interruption. Measure on the actual iPad speaker in an ordinary room as well as headphones. Track cold startup, warm startup, offline relaunch, memory and heat. A nicer voice alone will not fix turn-taking or error recovery; a month-long study with 15 older adults identifies conversation breakdowns as an important design problem. [Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02421).

**Next business step:** request Acapela and ReadSpeaker evaluation terms and exact German offline builds, without committing to a supplier. Ask whether the preview is the same neural voice delivered on iOS, whether it works indefinitely without online licence checks, whether App Store/managed distribution is permitted, how long the voice remains available, and how pricing changes with installations. No vendor was contacted and no additional engine was installed during this research.
